What this is

Haven Daily is a short brief, published every morning, on what affects the Port of Rotterdam and what it means for logistics planners. It shows at most five items, most severe first, each with what happened, a link to the original source and an interpretation of the impact for a planner. Quiet days get a shorter brief, never padded. Haven means port in Dutch and a safe place in English.

How a brief is made

  1. Collect, around 06:45 Amsterdam time. A scheduled job reads the sources below and skips items it has seen in the last 14 days.
  2. Filter. Fixed rules drop items older than 48 hours, ended port notices, old weekly roundups, off-topic headlines and any text that tries to instruct the AI. Items from Rotterdam-specific sources are kept. An AI model checks the rest and keeps only items with a clear link to the port, its hinterland routes or the shipping that calls there; a possible knock-on effect is not enough.
  3. Summarise. A second AI model writes a two-line summary, the impact for planners and a severity, and picks a place on the chart and "affects" tags from fixed lists.
  4. Status. Weather warnings, the Rhine level at Kaub and the strike flag.
  5. Publish. The brief goes online straight away. A day is never skipped.

Every step is automatic. Nobody checks a brief before it goes online. Mistakes found later are fixed with a visible correction; a brief is never changed silently. If a source fails, the rest is still published and the failure is listed under "How this brief was made".

Severity rules

High
Directly affects Rotterdam operations, with a timeframe: a strike at a terminal this week.
Medium
Affects a European route, carrier or hinterland connection that serves Rotterdam.
Low
Background or long-term news, or something already over (a closure lifted). An ended port notice, or a strike that doesn't affect Rotterdam, is always Low.
Unrated
The AI step failed: headline and link only, shown after rated items.

Within a severity, items with a summary come first. The impact for planners is an interpretation, set in a different typeface: based only on what the item says, with consequences written as possible, never certain. When the source doesn't support one, the item says Not enough information.

Headline-only sources are paywalled or their terms don't allow a summary. The AI rates their items from the headline alone, marked severity from the headline.

If the AI is down all morning, a reduced brief still goes out, with unrated headlines.

Weather warnings

Warnings are issued by KNMI, the Dutch weather service, and read once a day, around 06:45, from www.meteoalarm.org. A warning counts when its area covers any place on the chart, at sea included. This is a morning snapshot, not a live warning service ("Warnings at 06:45 · not live"); MeteoAlarm agreed on 23 November 2026 to this once-a-day snapshot. Haven Daily is an independent project, not a MeteoAlarm or KNMI service: always check the latest warnings on www.meteoalarm.org. The dial shows KNMI's own level and hazard next to ours and the time of issue, never the warning text.

How the strike flag works

Strikes are announced days ahead, so the flag reads the news of the last 14 days, and strikes dated today or later from up to 60 days back. Dates come from the source's own words; a date it doesn't state is never guessed.

  • The newest item about a strike decides. A cancellation brings the flag back to Normal.
  • Only strikes at the port or on its freight transport count, not bus, tram or metro strikes. Alert needs Rotterdam or a port place named; otherwise Watch.
  • A false alarm is better than a missed strike: only a confident match can lower a strike's level, and when a match is unsure both items are shown.
  • An ongoing strike with no newer report after two days drops to Watch, never to Normal by itself.
  • The flag links the item that decided it.

The AI models

  • Relevance check: {{ method.models.filter }}, one yes-or-no question per item the rules couldn't decide.
  • Summaries: {{ method.models.summary }}, chosen after a side-by-side test with {{ method.models.fallback }}, through Anthropic's Batch API at half the price. Items with no answer after {{ method.models.batch_wait_minutes }} minutes go to {{ method.models.fallback }}.
  • Every answer is checked in code (no copied runs of words, no overlong text): one retry, then the item is unrated.
  • All AI calls together are kept under €5 a month.

Copyright and article text

  • Every item links to its original source. Summaries are short and in the AI's own words, with no long quotes and nothing invented.
  • No article text is stored. An article is read in memory for its summary, then discarded.
  • Dutch items get an English summary and a Read in English link through Google Translate. Translated articles are never stored or republished here. Where it is switched on, the AI also writes an English headline, marked translated by AI, with the Dutch original below it.

Privacy

  • This site sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser, local storage included. No analytics or tracking of any kind.
  • Everything a page needs is served from this site, the map included, so opening a page contacts no other server. Links are not loads: another site is contacted only when you click. Read in English gives Google Translate the article's address.
  • The site is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub logs the IP address of every visitor for security (GitHub Pages: data collection; GitHub Privacy Statement). Haven Daily collects no personal data.

The chart

  • Land and water are drawn from OpenStreetMap data, prepared once, so no map service is used.
  • The lines in the water are decoration, a fixed distance from the shore: they are not depth contours.
  • Numbered squares are the day's items, at a place picked from a fixed list.
